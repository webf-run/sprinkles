A hero banner opens a page. A photo and a clear description help visitors
understand the page and decide what to do next. Everything comes in through
props, so each page can bring its own photo and words. For a band that closes a
page, use [CtaSection](/blocks/cta-section).

## Surface

`surface` picks the colour laid over the photo behind the copy. It is solid
under the text and fades out across the photo. It is named as in
[CtaSection](/blocks/cta-section) and the footer, so a page can use the same
three bands everywhere.

| Surface             | Band                           | Buttons                       | Use it for                                                 |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| `inverse` (default) | `surface-inverse`, a dark band | White, and a white outline    | Bright or busy photos; formal pages                        |
| `accent`            | `accent`, the product’s colour | White, and a white outline    | Pages that should feel like the brand                      |
| `muted`             | `surface-muted`, a pale band   | Accent, and an accent outline | Calm or warm photos; pages that already have a dark footer |

A banner has no colour for each kind of website. Its colours come from your
tokens: a charity that sets `--color-accent` to its green gets a green `accent`
banner, and the same banner is rose in a dance studio’s theme. Switch the theme
in the docs header and every banner on this page follows.

## Overlapping content

Children of `HeroBanner` sit across its bottom edge, half on the photo and
half on the page. The banner adds bottom padding to make room, so the
buttons never sit under them. It’s meant for a row of
[StatCards](/cards/stat-card), but anything works.

The example builds the row from existing components: a [Card](/layout/card)
holding one `StatCard` per entry of a `stats` array. Add or remove an entry
and the row shares its width equally (`@4xl:grid-flow-col @4xl:auto-cols-fr`),
with three to five reading best. The banner is an `@container`, so the row
follows the width the banner is given, not the window’s.

## Banners for different websites

A banner’s mood comes from the photo, the `surface`, the `align` (where the
text sits) and the `layout` (how photo and copy share space). The buttons
follow the band, so a call to action always stands out. A banner looks the same
in the light and the dark scheme.

## Actions

`image`, `title` and `primaryAction` are required. Add a useful description
with enough detail to read as a few lines. The photo is the banner’s
background by default, with the text over a readable scrim.

`primaryAction` is the one button most visitors should press. Add
`secondaryAction` for a quieter way forward, such as a visit before applying.
It’s an outline button, so the primary action still stands out. Every example
on this page uses both.

## Layout

The default `overlay` layout places the copy over the photo. Use
`layout='split'` when the image should have its own column beside the copy,
fading into the band. Use `layout='framed'` for a clean, rounded photo panel
with no fade, which suits busy photos. Set `contentSide='end'` to put the copy
on the right. Use `imageFit='contain'` when a portrait photo should remain fully
visible, and `imagePosition` to anchor it within its frame.

The layout follows the width the banner is given, not the window’s. Below
48rem every layout stacks the photo above the copy, and `mediaContent` stays
under the copy until the banner is 64rem wide. A banner in a narrow column
therefore stays readable at any window size.

## Image credits

Photos under CC BY ask for attribution. Pass `imageCredit` to print a small
credit in the banner’s corner:

```tsx
<HeroBanner
  imageCredit={{ text: 'Photo: Name · CC BY 2.0', href: 'https://…' }}
  …
/>
```

Every photo used in these examples is credited in
`public/images/cc/ATTRIBUTION.md`.

## Accessibility

- Renders a `<section>` labelled by its title, so it’s a region landmark with
  a name. The title is an `<h1>` by default; set `headingLevel={2}` on a page
  that already has one.
- The photo is decorative by default (`alt=""`). Say everything that matters in
  the text, never only in the picture. When the photo itself carries meaning,
  describe it with `imageAlt`.
- The copy’s contrast comes from the band, not the photo. The copy sits on a
  scrim that is at least 90% opaque and fades out only beyond the copy column.
  In a narrow banner the photo is a strip above the copy, so the copy sits on
  the plain band. Each surface pairs its band with the matching text token, a pair
  that `pnpm check:tokens` checks at 4.5:1. A very bright or very dark photo
  can’t lower it.
- Content you pass in `mediaContent` or `bottomContent` sits on the photo,
  outside the scrim. Give it a background of its own, such as a
  [Card](/layout/card) or a panel at least 90% opaque, rather than putting text
  straight on the picture.
- Buttons are links (`<a>`), 48 px tall, with the focus ring on keyboard
  focus only. They use static white, or the `accent` role on a `muted` band,
  and the banner fixes its colour scheme to light, so it looks the same in the
  light and the dark scheme.
- Overlapping content comes after the banner’s `<section>`, outside its
  labelled region, so it reads as content of its own.
