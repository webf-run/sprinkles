A hero banner introduces a page with a strong image, a clear heading, supporting
description and one or two actions. Use it to help visitors understand the page
and decide what to do next. For a closing call to action, use
[CtaSection](/blocks/cta-section).

## Basic usage

The image, title and primary action are the essential parts of a banner.
Add a description to explain the value of the page, and a secondary action
when visitors need another route.

```tsx
<HeroBanner
  image='/images/your-hero-image.jpg'
  title='Make your next step count.'
  description='A concise description that explains what visitors can discover.'
  primaryAction={{ label: 'Explore programmes', href: '/programmes' }}
  secondaryAction={{ label: 'Contact us', href: '/contact' }}
/>
```

## Actions and supporting content

`primaryAction` is the main call to action. Add `secondaryAction` for a
quieter alternative, such as learning more before making a decision.

Use `note` for a short reassurance below the buttons. `mediaContent` places
supporting content in the photo area, while `bottomContent` places content
along the lower edge of the banner. Children can overlap the bottom edge of
the banner; this works well for a row of [StatCards](/cards/stat-card).

See the [overlapping content example](/blocks/hero-banner-examples#overlapping-content)
for a complete implementation.

## Layout

- `overlay` places the copy over the photo and is the default layout.
- `split` gives the image and copy their own columns. The image stacks above
  the text on small screens.
- `framed` places the photo in its own rounded panel beside the copy.

Use `contentSide='end'` to place the copy on the right in split layouts.
`imageFit` controls how the photo fills its area, and `imagePosition` anchors
the subject within the frame.

## Variants

The `variant` prop selects a semantic colour treatment for the banner band
and its actions. Available variants are `inverse`, `neutral`, `positive`,
`informative`, `indigo`, `highlight` and `light`. Choose the variant that
supports the content and contrast of the image. The component uses semantic
theme tokens, so site-level theme changes are reflected automatically.

The `imageTone` prop can apply a `natural`, `cool` or `warm` treatment to
the photo independently of the variant.

## Image credits

Photos under licences such as CC BY require attribution. Pass `imageCredit`
to display a small credit in the banner:

```tsx
<HeroBanner
  imageCredit={{ text: 'Photo: Name · CC BY 2.0', href: 'https://…' }}
  …
/>
```

Credits for the photos used in the examples are listed in
`public/images/cc/ATTRIBUTION.md`.

## Accessibility

- The banner renders a `<section>` labelled by its title, creating a named
  region landmark. The title is an `<h1>` by default; set `headingLevel={2}`
  when the page already has a level-one heading.
- The photo is decorative (`alt=""`). Include important information in the
  text rather than relying on the image.
- In the overlay layout, a colour scrim sits between the photo and text.
  Each variant pairs its band with the matching foreground token. Check
  especially bright photos to ensure text remains readable.
- Actions render as links and provide visible keyboard focus. Keep action
  labels descriptive so their purpose is clear out of context.
- Overlapping children are placed after the banner section, outside its
  labelled region, so they are read as separate page content.
