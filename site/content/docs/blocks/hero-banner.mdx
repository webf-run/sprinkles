A hero banner opens a page. A photo and a clear description help visitors
understand the page and decide what to do next. Everything comes in through
props, so each page can bring its own photo and words. For a band that closes a page, use
[CtaSection](/blocks/cta-section).

## Overlapping content

Children of `HeroBanner` sit across its bottom edge, half on the photo and
half on the page. The banner adds bottom padding to make room, so the
buttons never sit under them. It's meant for a row of
[StatCards](/cards/stat-card), but anything works.

The example builds the row from existing components: a [Card](/layout/card)
holding one `StatCard` per entry of a `stats` array. Add or remove an entry
and the row shares its width equally (`@4xl:grid-flow-col @4xl:auto-cols-fr`),
with three to five reading best. The banner is an `@container`, so the row
follows the width it's given, not the window's.

## Banners for different websites

A banner's mood comes from the photo, the `variant` (the colour of the band),
the `align` (where the text sits) and the `layout` (how photo and copy share
space). The buttons follow
the band, so a call to action always stands out. Every variant is a semantic
colour, so a site that re-skins its tokens re-skins its banners too, and a
banner looks the same in the light and the dark scheme.

| Variant       | Band                  | Buttons                       | Fits                        |
| ------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| `inverse`     | Deep, the default     | White, and a white outline    | Education, corporate        |
| `positive`    | Green                 | White, and a white outline    | Charities, health, nature   |
| `indigo`      | Deep indigo           | White, and a white outline    | Software, restaurants, tech |
| `informative` | Blue                  | White, and a white outline    | Finance, public services    |
| `neutral`     | Charcoal              | White, and a white outline    | Travel, media, portfolios   |
| `highlight`   | Cream, with dark text | Amber, and a neutral outline  | Food, retail, events        |
| `light`       | Pale, with dark text  | Accent, and an accent outline | Clinics, wellness, family   |

Pair any variant with `imageTone='cool'` for a blue wash over the photo. The
wash keeps every banner on the same calm, cool palette while the variant,
`align` and `layout` give each site its own composition.

## Actions

`image`, `title` and `primaryAction` are required. Add a useful description
with enough detail to read as a few lines. The photo is the banner's
background by default, with the text over a readable colour overlay.

`primaryAction` is the one button most visitors should press. Add
`secondaryAction` for a quieter way forward, such as a visit before applying.
It's an outline button, so the primary action still stands out. Every example
on this page uses both.

## Layout

The default `overlay` layout places the copy over the photo. Use
`layout='split'` when the image should have its own column beside the copy,
fading into the band. Use `layout='framed'` for a clean, rounded photo panel
with no fade, which suits busy photos.
Set `contentSide='end'` in a split layout to put the copy on the right and
`mediaContent` on the left. Use `imageFit='contain'` when a portrait photo
should remain fully visible, and `imagePosition` to anchor it within its frame.
The split layout stacks the image above the text on small screens.

See the [real estate](#real-estate), [online learning](#online-learning) and
[grocery](#grocery-and-farm-boxes) examples for `split`, and the
[clinic](#clinic-and-wellness) and [bakery](#bakery-and-cafe) examples for `framed`.

## Image credits

Photos under CC BY ask for attribution. Pass `imageCredit` to print a small
credit in the banner's corner:

```tsx
<HeroBanner
  imageCredit={{ text: 'Photo: Name · CC BY 2.0', href: 'https://…' }}
  …
/>
```

Every photo used in these examples is credited in
`public/images/cc/ATTRIBUTION.md`.

## Accessibility

- Renders a `<section>` labelled by its title, so it's a region landmark with
  a name. The title is an `<h1>` by default; set `headingLevel={2}` on a page
  that already has one.
- The photo is decorative (`alt=""`). Say everything that matters in the
  text, never only in the picture.
- In the overlay layout, a coloured scrim sits between the photo and the text,
  so the title and description stay readable. Each variant pairs its band with
  the matching `-foreground` token, a pair that `pnpm check:tokens` checks at
  4.5:1. Very bright photos still deserve a check.
- Buttons are links (`<a>`), 48 px tall, with the focus ring on keyboard
  focus only. They use static white or black, or the `highlight` and `accent`
  roles, and the banner fixes its colour scheme to light, so it looks the
  same in the light and the dark scheme.
- Overlapping content comes after the banner's `<section>`, outside its
  labelled region, so it reads as content of its own.
