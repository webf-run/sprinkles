A hero banner opens a page. A photo sets the scene while a clear, informative
description explains what the visitor will find and why it matters. Each
example pairs its copy, colour and layout with the needs of its website. For a
band that closes a page, use
[CtaSection](/blocks/cta-section).

## Overlapping content

Children of `HeroBanner` sit across its bottom edge, half on the photo and
half on the page. The banner adds bottom padding to make room, so the
buttons never sit under them. It's meant for a row of
[StatCards](/cards/stat-card), but anything works.

The example builds the row from existing components: a [Card](/layout/card)
holding one `StatCard` per entry of a `stats` array. Add or remove an entry
and the row shares its width equally (`@4xl:grid-flow-col @4xl:auto-cols-fr`),
with three to five reading best. The banner is an `@container`, so the row
follows the width it's given, not the window's.

## Banners for different websites

A banner's mood comes from its photo, `variant`, `align` and `layout`. The
default `overlay` layout places copy over a colour scrim; `split` gives the
photo its own column. `mediaContent` places a panel in the right column over
the photo, which works well for an appointment chooser, account summary or
interactive menu.
Add an `eyebrow`, shown as a `Badge`, for context, and shape the description
around what visitors need to know. Every variant resolves a colour token, so a
site that re-skins its tokens re-skins its banners too.

| Variant       | Band                 | Buttons                       | Fits                      |
| ------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| `inverse`     | Dark, the default    | Orange, and a white outline   | Education, corporate      |
| `positive`    | Green                | White, and a white outline    | Charities, health, nature |
| `indigo`      | Deep indigo          | White, and a white outline    | Software, technology      |
| `informative` | Blue                 | White, and a white outline    | Finance, public services  |
| `neutral`     | Charcoal             | White, and a white outline    | Travel, media, portfolios |
| `highlight`   | Warm, with dark text | Black, and a black outline    | Food, retail, events      |
| `light`       | Pale, with dark text | Accent, and an accent outline | Clinics, wellness, family |

## Primary action

`image`, `title` and `primaryAction` are required. Give `description` enough
detail to explain the offer in roughly three lines or more at desktop widths.
The photo is decorative, so choose one that supports the page without carrying
information visitors need to understand the offer.

## Descriptive copy

Use the description to replace scan-only checklists with useful context.
Explain what the organisation offers, how it works and what a visitor can
expect next. Keep the copy specific to the site: a bank can describe account
tools and support, while a university can explain teaching and work
experience. Aim for at least three lines at desktop widths.

## Accessibility

- Renders a `<section>` labelled by its title, so it's a region landmark with
  a name. The title is an `<h1>` by default; set `headingLevel={2}` on a page
  that already has one.
- The photo is decorative (`alt=""`). Say everything that matters in the
  text, never only in the picture.
- A coloured overlay sits between the photo and the text in the `overlay`
  layout. Each variant pairs its band with
  the matching `-foreground` token, a pair that `pnpm check:tokens` checks at
  4.5:1. Very bright photos still deserve a check.
- Buttons are links (`<a>`), 48 px tall, with the focus ring on keyboard
  focus only. They use static white or black, or the `highlight` and `accent`
  roles, and the banner fixes its colour scheme to light, so it looks the
  same in the light and the dark scheme.
- Overlapping content comes after the banner's `<section>`, outside its
  labelled region, so it reads as content of its own.
